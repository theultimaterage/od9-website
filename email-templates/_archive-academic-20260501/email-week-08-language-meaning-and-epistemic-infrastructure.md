# Capture the language, capture the thought

Hey {{FIRST_NAME}},

There's a short list of words in the English language that have been weaponized so consistently, by so many sides, that they're almost unusable in serious conversation. You can probably name a few:

- **"Freedom."** Means everything from "leave me alone" to "you must do what I say."
- **"Fairness."** Means whatever the speaker wants the next sentence to mean.
- **"Natural."** Has been used to defend slavery, hierarchy, gender roles, dietary advice, and chemtrails.
- **"Extremist."** Increasingly just means "person whose politics I find inconvenient."
- **"Common sense."** Almost always means "obvious to me; you must be stupid or evil if you disagree."
- **"Innovation."** Means "any new product I want you to buy."

You probably have your own list. Maybe one of these is a word you *use* — and you've been using it carefully. The problem isn't that the word is bad. The problem is that other people have hollowed it out.

When the words people use to think with get hollowed out, **the thinking itself gets harder**. Not because people get dumber, but because they're operating with broken tools.

## Why this matters

**Ch.9** of the Manifesto (*Information Ecology*) and **Ch.11** make the case directly: **language is the substrate of coordination**. Capture the language, capture the thinking. Capture the thinking, capture the coordination. Capture the coordination, capture the future.

This is why authoritarian movements always invest heavily in redefining basic vocabulary, why advertising campaigns try to *own* words ("just do it," "think different"), why platforms redefine "engagement" to mean things that aren't engagement, why every regime in history has had a cleanup crew for inconvenient terminology.

It's not paranoid — it's just how power works. The defense isn't to refuse the words. The defense is to **be unusually precise about what we mean by ours**, and to know when other people's vocabulary has been compromised.

OD9's bet is that we can hold our key terms — *coordination*, *consciousness*, *flourishing*, *contribution*, *Type 1*, *ASCEND* — to a higher standard of precision than the surrounding noise. We won't always succeed. But the discipline matters.

## This week's question

Sunday's deep-dive QOTD:

> *Name a word that gets weaponized so often it's almost unusable now — "freedom," "fairness," "natural," "extremist," whatever. If you had to rewrite the definition for the next 100 years, what would it say?*

Rewriting a definition forces you to take a position. That's the point.

3. **Bring your rewritten definition to Discord.** The disagreements about which words even *belong* on the list are usually more interesting than the rewrites themselves.
