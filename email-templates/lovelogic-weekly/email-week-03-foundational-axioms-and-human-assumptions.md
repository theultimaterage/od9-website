# Name one thing you treat as obvious

Hey {{FIRST_NAME}},

Every coordination system smuggles in axioms — assumptions so basic that the people inside the system stop seeing them. Religion, science, capitalism, democracy, OD9, your family, your friend group: all of them.

The assumptions you can name aren't dangerous. You can argue with them. You can update them when evidence shifts.

The dangerous ones are the ones nobody sees — the ones that quietly shape every decision and that nobody can articulate, because they're the *background*.

Naming them is hard. Replacing them is harder. Skipping the work is how civilizations fail.

## What the Manifesto admits

[**Ch.2** of the Manifesto](https://offda9.com/curriculum/observer/the-evidence-standard.php) (*Theoretical Foundations and Methodology*) is unusually honest for a foundational text — it says straight up which of OD9's principles are axiomatic (we treat them as starting points) and which are provisional (we expect them to change as evidence accumulates).

That distinction matters. Most belief systems treat *every* principle as eternal. OD9 doesn't. The reason is in **Ch.6** (*Responsible Transformation Frameworks*): if your framework can't update, it's already dead — it just hasn't noticed yet.

So when we say "consciousness development is co-equal with technological development," that's axiomatic. When we say "tier-based progression is the right shape for membership," that's provisional. We could be wrong. The thing is to know which is which.

## This week's question

Sunday's deep-dive QOTD turns this back on us:

> *Name one thing OD9 (or the manifesto) treats as obviously true that you're not 100% sure about. What evidence would change your mind?*

This is one of the hardest questions in the campaign. Most members will skip it. The ones who don't are the ones who actually move the project forward.

## Three things to do this week

1. **Try the question on yourself first.** What do *you* treat as obvious that you've never had to defend? It's harder than it sounds.
2. **Read [The Evidence Standard](https://offda9.com/curriculum/observer/the-evidence-standard.php) and [Responsible Transformation](https://offda9.com/curriculum/observer/responsible-transformation.php) in the Codex.** Pay attention to which claims are presented as axioms vs. arguments. The distinction is unusual.
3. **Bring one challenge to the Discord.** Not as a debate prompt — as an honest "I think we might be wrong about X." Members who've lived through other movements will tell you: this is the only thing that keeps an ideology honest.
