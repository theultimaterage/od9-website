# Who tries to co-opt it first?

Hey {{FIRST_NAME}},

This week's exercise is paranoid. That's the point.

Imagine OD9 ever actually gets traction. Not viral — but real. 50,000 active members. Discord channels full of working groups. Pioneer-led initiatives shipping. Funded by a thousand Patreons that can't easily be cancelled by any single actor. Real cultural footprint — Kardashev-thinking starting to show up in mainstream discourse, members embedded in institutions, the manifesto cited in academic papers.

What's the **first institution** that tries to co-opt it?

Be cynical. Specific is better than abstract.

Some candidates I've seen people propose:

- **A political party.** They notice OD9 members vote a certain way at higher-than-baseline rates and try to incorporate the vocabulary into platform language. Plausible if we're not careful.
- **A tech company.** They sponsor a "Kardashev Innovation Lab" or fund select Pioneers in exchange for early access to research. Captures the talent.
- **A religious or quasi-religious organization.** They map ASCEND onto an existing soteriology and recruit OD9 members as proto-converts. Not as far-fetched as it sounds — every charismatic framework attracts wrappers.
- **A university or think tank.** Picks up Wax5 of the manifesto, builds a degree program, pulls the academic credibility into their existing institutional brand. Captures the research output.
- **The defense or intelligence sector.** They notice the coordination-design work has dual-use applications and quietly fund research streams. Hardest one to defend against because the offers are real.
- **An MLM or scammy-pyramid version.** Someone clones the tier system, the language, the aesthetics — and runs it for personal gain. The kind of thing that destroys movements by association.
- **The original founders themselves.** Worth saying. The bigger the project gets, the bigger the temptation to optimize for personal continuation rather than mission.

None of these are paranoid speculation. All of them have happened to comparable movements within the last 50 years. Naming them now is how you build the antibodies.

## What the Manifesto pushes on

**Ch.49** of the Manifesto (*ASCEND Implementation*) — which is the chapter that this entire bot is the implementation of — takes a position most reform movements skip: **adoption strategy and capture-resistance design are co-equal with the design of the thing being adopted.** A coordination mechanism that scales without thinking about who's going to try to capture it will be captured. Always. By the most resourceful actor whose interests are best served by capture.

So the work is: design assuming capture attempts WILL happen, name the most likely vectors in advance, and build the institutional habits that make each vector harder than the next-best alternative for the would-be capturer. Not "stop them from trying" — "make trying not worth it."
