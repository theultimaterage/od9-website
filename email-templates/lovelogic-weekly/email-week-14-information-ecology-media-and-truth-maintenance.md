# What actually changes your mind?

Hey {{FIRST_NAME}},

Quick exercise. In the last 30 days, name one thing you believed and later updated.

Could be small ("turns out the tax credit I thought existed doesn't"). Could be large ("my read on this person was wrong"). Could be in any domain — politics, work, science, a relationship, a worldview.

Got one? Hold it.

Now answer: **what caused the update?**

- New evidence?
- A specific person?
- A single line you read or heard?
- An experience that contradicted your model?
- Time and reflection?
- An argument that landed?

Most people, doing this honestly, find that their actual belief-update mechanism is *not* what they'd guess. We tell ourselves we update on evidence. The honest answer is usually that we update on people we trust, on experiences that hit us emotionally, and on framings that make a previously-incoherent set of facts suddenly coherent. Pure data — divorced from people, experience, and framing — almost never moves anyone.

This isn't a flaw. It's how human cognition actually works. The flaw is *pretending* it's not, because then you can't design information systems that account for the actual mechanism.

## What the Manifesto says

**Ch.14** of the Manifesto and **Ch.39** make a sharp claim: **the dominant information channels in a society shape what's "true" for most people in it, and they were never designed to optimize for truth — they were designed to optimize for engagement, profit, or political control.**

That's not paranoia. It's the architecture. Cable news optimizes for emotional retention. Social media optimizes for engagement. Most academic publishing optimizes for novelty and prestige. Search engines optimize for ad revenue. None of these are *opposed* to truth, but truth is at best a secondary outcome — and when secondary outcomes conflict with primary ones, the primary wins.

A Type-I society needs information infrastructure that has truth as the *primary* target. We don't have it. Building it is generational work. In the meantime, the best individual move is to **understand your own belief-update mechanism well enough to know when you're being moved by something other than evidence.**

That's the value of the exercise above. It's a self-audit.
