# Five things, no platitudes

Hey {{FIRST_NAME}},

You have one job this week: write down five things you think every human alive in 2050 should be guaranteed.

Not aspirations. Not values. **Five concrete things, deliverable by infrastructure**, that every human gets just for existing.

You can't put "love" on the list. You can't put "happiness." You can't put "dignity" — too vague to deliver. The list has to be specific enough that a competent civilization could actually build the system that provides them.

This is harder than people expect. Most lists collapse into one of two failure modes:

**Failure mode 1:** Too vague. "Education." "Healthcare." "Safety." OK — *what specifically*? Reading at what level? Treatment for which conditions? Safe from whom?

**Failure mode 2:** Too libertarian-flavored. "Property." "Free speech." "Self-defense." Those aren't deliverables — those are *constraints* on the rest of the list. Useful, but not a guarantee in themselves.

The real list is hard to write. Try it.

## Why this matters

[**Ch.7** of the Manifesto](https://offda9.com/curriculum/observer/the-solution-framework.php) (*The Solution Framework*) makes a sharp claim: **a "right" that can't be delivered by infrastructure is a sentiment, not a right**. Rights without delivery mechanisms are how regimes get away with putting beautiful language in their constitutions and then doing whatever they want.

That's not cynicism. That's design. If we're going to build something that actually produces flourishing across billions of people, we have to be operational. Five things. Specific. Buildable.

(**Ch.5** sets up the urgency: we're already failing to deliver the implicit baselines — clean water, basic nutrition, predictable shelter, freedom from preventable disease — to a non-trivial slice of the species. That's the problem we're trying to not reproduce at scale.)

## This week's question

Sunday's deep-dive QOTD:

> *If every human alive in 2050 was guaranteed exactly five things — and only five — what would you put on the list? (No "love" or "happiness" — the list has to be deliverable by infrastructure.)*

Your five tells us a lot about what kind of civilization you're actually trying to build.

## Three things to do this week

1. **Write your five.** Actually write them. Sit with the list overnight. Revise. The first draft is almost always wrong.
2. **Compare your list to one other person's** — partner, friend, anyone willing. The disagreements are where the design questions live.
3. **Drop your five in the Discord** Sunday when the QOTD lands. One of the most useful artifacts we'll generate this year is the aggregated list.
