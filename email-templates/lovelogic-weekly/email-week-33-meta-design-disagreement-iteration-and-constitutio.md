# What changed

Hey {{FIRST_NAME}},

We made it.

33 weeks. 33 categories. About 50,000 words of prompts, scenarios, manifesto cross-references, and Sunday QOTDs. If you read most of them — even if you only engaged some of them — you've spent meaningful time inside a way of thinking that almost nobody around you uses.

The question for this final email is the only one that matters:

**What changed?**

Not "did you learn things." Not "what did you find interesting." What CHANGED. About how you read the news, how you talk to your family, how you think about your job, what you say "yes" or "no" to, what you find tolerable that used to feel normal, what you find normal that used to seem extreme.

Most people, doing this honestly, can name at least one thing. Some people can name several. Some people can't name any — and that's also data: maybe the campaign reached you at the wrong moment, maybe the framework didn't land, maybe you read but didn't engage.

All three are honest answers.

## What the framework was for

**Ch.1, Ch.2, Ch.6, Ch.38** of the Manifesto, plus the Preface, take a position about meta-design that's load-bearing for everything else: **a coordination framework that can't update itself in light of new evidence is a dead framework, no matter how impressive its first draft is.**

That's the whole point of the campaign you just read. It wasn't to convert you to a position. It was to give you 33 entry points into a way of asking questions, with the expectation that the *answers* will keep changing as you and the rest of the community learn.

The answers we wrote together this year are not the final ones. The 30-something Sunday QOTDs we ran in #question-of-the-day are not the final question set. The manifesto itself, even — the 67 chapters of it — is explicitly described in **Ch.6** as a draft, intended to be revised by Type-I-aware successors.

This means the most important practice we can build, as a community, is **the practice of updating well.** Not flip-flopping. Not "rebranding." Genuinely updating: holding a previous belief, encountering new evidence or argument, naming the gap, and moving — explicitly — to a better-justified position.

Almost no movement does this well. The ones that do are the ones that survive past their founders.

## What's next

Some practical things:
- The campaign starts again in 2027 — but the rewrite cadence is rolling, so each year's emails will get sharper as the community itself sharpens. Members who've been around will see the difference. That's intentional.
- The Sunday QOTDs continue every Sunday. New ones get suggested via `/qotd submit` and reviewed by Pioneers and admins.
- Tier-up emails, achievement emails, and streak milestones now flow automatically through the bot → website → email pipeline (built this April; see `/pipeline_health` if you're an admin).
- Patreon, Discord governance, and the Pioneer-led initiatives keep moving in the background. They take longer because they touch more people — but the work is real.

Some less-practical things:
- If even one prompt in this campaign genuinely shifted how you see something, **forward this final email to the person who'd benefit most from going through it from week 1.** The community doesn't grow by us shouting; it grows by individual members vouching.
- If you've been reading without participating, **drop a one-line answer to "what changed" in #question-of-the-day this week.** Even one line. The community is genuinely curious — and lurkers who suddenly speak up are some of the most interesting people we've ever had.
- If you've been participating, **thank you.** Movements don't survive on their founders. They survive on the members who stay, ask hard questions, take the work seriously, and refuse to let the project drift into easy mode.
