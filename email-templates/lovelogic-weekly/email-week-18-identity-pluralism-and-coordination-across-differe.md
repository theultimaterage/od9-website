# Without one of you losing

Hey {{FIRST_NAME}},

Name a group of people you find genuinely hard to coordinate with.

Not "people I disagree with politically." Not "people on the other side of the timeline." Actual coordination — getting work done in the same room, agreeing on what constitutes evidence, designing institutions together, building anything that lasts.

Could be:
- A religious community whose epistemics you find baffling
- A political coalition whose tactics you can't stomach
- A generational cohort whose values feel alien
- A culture whose communication style hits you as hostile when it isn't
- A profession whose worldview is so domain-shaped you can't bridge it
- The blood-relatives you can't talk to past 20 minutes

Now the harder question: **what's ONE thing that group and you both actually want?**

If you can find one — even one — then coordination is *possible*. Painful, slow, full of meta-arguments about what counts as fair. But possible. If you can't find one, it's worth asking honestly: do you actually want to coordinate, or do you want them to lose?

This is the most fundamental design question facing any civilization-scale project, OD9 included: **can we build the thing without forcing someone to lose?**

## What the Manifesto pushes on

**Ch.22** of the Manifesto and **Ch.25** take a position that's honestly uncomfortable for everyone, regardless of where they sit politically: **a successful Type-I civilization will have to coordinate across kinds of difference that we don't currently know how to coordinate across — including kinds of difference that the present moment treats as zero-sum.**

Not "tolerance" in the soft sense. Not "agree to disagree" while continuing to fight. Something harder: institutions and habits that let people who'd never share a meal still share a working coordination mechanism on a specific shared interest. Federalism is one design move toward this. Subsidiarity is another. Pluralist constitutional design is another. None of them solve it. All of them are necessary infrastructure.

The OD9 bet is that if the people inside the project can't model this coordination at small scale — across the differences inside our own community — we'll never build the institutions that do it at planetary scale.
