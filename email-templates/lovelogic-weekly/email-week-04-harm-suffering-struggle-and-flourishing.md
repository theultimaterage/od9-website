# What counts as harm?

Hey {{FIRST_NAME}},

Walk into your old high school today. Just imagine it. Walk the halls. Look at the cafeteria. Sit in the back of a classroom for ten minutes.

Now tell me: how many things being done to those students would you call **harm**?

Not "uncomfortable." Not "annoying." Not even "bad teaching." Actual harm — damage to a developing human being that the institution either causes or refuses to prevent.

Most people, doing this honestly, can name three to five things in under five minutes. Standardized testing as the central organizing metric. Cell-phone surveillance dressed up as classroom management. Lunch periods so short they're physiologically unhealthy. Teachers stretched so thin they can't catch the kid who's drowning. Suspensions that statistically harm the kid more than the behavior they were "addressing."

But here's the harder part: a few of those things you named are *actually fine*. Maybe necessary. The job of OD9-adjacent thinking is to draw the line carefully, not just sweep everything into the harm column to feel virtuous.

## Why this matters more than usual

[**Ch.5**](https://offda9.com/curriculum/observer/the-system-in-crisis.php) of the Manifesto (*The System in Crisis*) makes a claim a lot of comfortable people don't want to hear: **most institutional harm in the modern world isn't intent — it's the byproduct of systems optimizing for things other than human flourishing**. Schools optimize for measurable test outcomes. Hospitals optimize for billing codes. Cities optimize for property tax base. Apps optimize for engagement.

When the optimization target isn't "humans flourish," harm becomes background. Invisible. Normal. And then nobody is responsible because nobody chose it.

If we're going to build something that doesn't reproduce that pattern, we have to be ruthless about defining harm — *operationally*, not as moral theater. Otherwise we'll build a new system that optimizes for the wrong thing and end up at the same dumpster fire from a different direction.
