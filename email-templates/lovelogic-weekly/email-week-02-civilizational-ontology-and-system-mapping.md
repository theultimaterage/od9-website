# Map a system you're already inside

Hey {{FIRST_NAME}},

Pick a system you've been complaining about lately. Could be your job, your kid's school, the algorithm on whatever app you keep deleting and re-installing, your city, the neighborhood Facebook group, *anything*. Got one? Hold it in mind.

Now answer: **what's the unit?**

Is it the individual — the person who clocks in, the kid who sits in the desk, the user who opens the app? Is it the household? The shift? The class? The grade? The school? The district? The company? The platform's parent corporation? The regulatory body? The shareholder pool?

Because here's the thing: most "systems we hate" are stacks of nested systems. And the leverage point — the place where one push actually changes the behavior of the whole thing — is almost never where you'd guess.

## Why this matters

[**Ch.4**](https://offda9.com/curriculum/observer/the-twin-singularities.php) of the Manifesto runs through this in detail (it's called *The Great Awakening*). The argument is that humanity keeps trying to fix problems at the wrong layer. We try to fix climate at the level of individual consumer habits when the levers are at the regulatory and infrastructure layer. We try to fix loneliness at the individual therapy layer when the levers are in zoning law and labor scheduling. We try to fix misinformation at the individual fact-check layer when the levers are in the recommendation algorithms.

The mismatch isn't accidental. It's the *easiest layer to organize people at* — which is the individual — almost never matches the *layer where the actual leverage lives*.

So if we're going to build something that doesn't fail the same way, we have to get serious about ontology — about what the units actually *are* in any system we touch.

## This week's question

Sunday's deep-dive QOTD:

> *Pick the unit of human life you trust most to make hard decisions: the individual, the household, the neighborhood, the city, the nation, or the species. Defend your pick — and name the kind of decision your unit handles **worst**.*

Most people have never named which layer they actually trust. The answer is more interesting than it sounds.
