# 90 seconds

Hey {{FIRST_NAME}},

Stranger sits down next to you on a flight. They notice you reading something OD9-related. They ask, casually: "What's that?"

You have **90 seconds** before they go back to their phone. They're not hostile. They're not particularly curious either. They're being polite.

**What do you actually say?**

This is the most honest test of whether your understanding of OD9 has compressed yet. If you can't pitch it in 90 seconds to a stranger, one of two things is true:
1. You don't actually understand it well enough to compress it. (Common. Fixable.)
2. The framework hasn't compressed itself well enough yet. (Also common. Also fixable.)

Both are useful diagnostics. Either way, sitting down and writing your actual 90-second pitch is one of the most useful things any OD9 member can do.

Constraints:
- 90 seconds out loud, not 90 seconds of reading. About 200-220 spoken words.
- The stranger is intelligent but unaware. No jargon. No "Kardashev" without immediately explaining what you mean. No "ASCEND" without a one-line gloss.
- They're going to make ONE judgment based on what you say: do they find you a) interesting, b) crank-y, c) boring. Aim for a.
- You don't have to convert them. You just have to leave them with one true sentence about what OD9 is.

Some structural patterns that work:

- **Problem → frame → why this is different.** "There's a thing called the coordination problem — it's why every group of smart individuals collectively makes worse decisions than the dumbest one of them. OD9 is a community of people trying to design coordination mechanisms that don't have that failure mode. We're explicit that we might be wrong. Most movements aren't."
- **Moment of recognition.** "You ever notice how every institution we have was designed for problems we no longer have? OD9 is the place I go to think about what the next institutions actually need to look like."
- **Founding myth.** "There's a guy in Chicago who got tired of waiting for someone with a Harvard credential to design the future. So he wrote a book — actually a 67-chapter book — on what civilization should be optimizing for if we want a shot at not destroying ourselves. OD9 is the community that grew up around it."

None of those are right for you. The point is to write yours.

## What the Manifesto pushes on

**Ch.5** and **Ch.67** of the Manifesto take a position about communication that's worth treating seriously: **a movement that can't compress itself into a sentence isn't a movement, it's a manifesto.** Manifestos die. Movements survive precisely because their members can carry them in their heads, articulate them on demand, defend them in 90 seconds.

That doesn't mean dumb-down. It means do the cognitive work yourself, in advance, so you don't fumble in the moment. Every member who can pitch OD9 in 90 seconds is a node. Every member who can't is a bottleneck.
